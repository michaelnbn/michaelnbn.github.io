Pit and pipe design for single dwelling unit developments

NBN requires a dedicated lead-in conduit from an NBN pit into each premises. We design the pits, routes and conduit sizes and prepare the design for NBN review.

Reviewed 4 October 2026 against NBN's published guidance

What the design covers

  • Pit locations, using the pit types NBN allows
  • Conduit routes, sizes and lengths from the street network to each premises
  • A dedicated lead-in conduit from a pit into each premises
  • Locations for the premises connection device at each lot
  • Existing roads and telecom pits around the site, documented in the design

Rules that shape an SDU design

For SDUs, including duplexes and triplexes, NBN requires one P20 service drop conduit per premises.

The furthest service drop conduit run from a pit to the property boundary entry must not exceed 25 m.

Where practical, pits stay out of driveways and roadways, and sit offset 350 mm from the street-facing property boundary.

NBN reviews each design through the nbn New Developments portal before construction.

One pit, several lots

A single pit can serve a group of neighbouring lots, commonly around four to six depending on the pit type and layout. Each lot still gets its own conduit from the pit, so every premises has a dedicated path.

What we need to start

  • Site address and state or territory
  • Number of lots and the stage of the development
  • CAD (DWG) and/or PDF development plans showing roads, lot boundaries and existing services
  • Your NBN application reference, if you have one
  • Dial Before You Dig or site survey results, if you have them

Small developments

For small developments of up to 20 premises, NBN accepts designs as PDF files, and using NBN's AutoCAD drafting tool is optional.
