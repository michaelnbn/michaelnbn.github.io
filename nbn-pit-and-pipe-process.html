How the NBN new developments process works

Pit and pipe design is the first of six stages on the way to NBN's Certificate of Practical Completion.

Reviewed 4 October 2026 against NBN's published guidance

The six stages

  1. Design. The pit and pipe design is prepared and uploaded to the nbn New Developments portal for review. This is the stage we handle.
  2. Build. Pits and conduit are installed to the approved design.
  3. As-builts. As-built designs and the Notice of Practical Completion are uploaded.
  4. Remediate if required. Any corrections NBN asks for are made.
  5. Connection boxes. Connection boxes are pre-installed, ideally at frame stage.
  6. Inspection and certificate. NBN inspects the work and issues the Certificate of Practical Completion.

What NBN asks for in a design

NBN's guidance for small developments of up to 20 premises lists these requirements.

The design is submitted as a PDF through the nbn New Developments portal for review before construction. A DWG file made with NBN's drafting tool is optional.

Only pit types that meet NBN's standard are used, and they are placed away from trafficable areas and at least 15 m from electrical transformers.

Conduit is white rigid PVC. Service drop conduits are limited to 25 m with no more than three 90 degree bends.

The developer confirms a Dial Before You Dig or site survey check, documents the surrounding roads and existing telecom pits, and gives a premises count that matches the stage application.

Who does the design

The developer is responsible for the design. NBN encourages developers to use an industry professional or an NBN trained new developments supplier so the design is right first time and certification is faster. NBN's training is free, and trained consultants are listed in NBN's public directory.

The standard we design to

NBN publishes its requirements as the New Developments pit and conduit standard, NBN-TE-CTO-194. We check the current version before each job.
