NBN pits and conduit: a plain guide for developers

Which pit types NBN allows, what each conduit size is for, how many conduits each pit can take, and the clearances that decide where pits can go.

Reviewed 26 October 2026 against NBN-TE-CTO-194 Revision 13.0

In short: NBN pit and pipe uses white PVC conduit in three sizes. P100 and P50 run along and across the street, and P20 runs from a pit to each house. Pits come in a handful of standard sizes, from the small Type 2 to a manhole, and each size has a fixed limit on how many conduits can enter each end. Pits must stay clear of transformers, poles, driveways and kerbs.

Conduit sizes and what they do

NBN conduit sizes (white PVC to AS/NZS 1477)
SizeMain uses
P100 (100 mm)Distribution and local network conduit along streets, road crossings for the local network, service drop to vertical MDUs over 60 premises
P50 (50 mm)Lateral conduits across streets, laneway crossings, lead-in to MDUs of 2 to 60 premises, internal networks in super lots of 4 to 24 premises
P20 (20 mm)Service drop to each SDU premises and small commercial premises, lead-ins in super lots of three or fewer premises

Every conduit carries a synthetic draw cord of at least 3 mm with a minimum breaking force of 90 kg, and is sealed at both ends.

This table is why pit choice matters on a subdivision. A Type 5 boundary pit carrying one P100 through the street can only take four P20 service drops at that end. NBN's standard also allocates lots to a service drop or boundary pit in groups of typically four, up to eight.

Where pits can and cannot go

At least 15 m from pad-mounted transformers and poles with transformers.

3 m recommended from other poles and streetlights, with 1 m as the minimum.

Not in any area that may be subject to earth potential rise (EPR) from the power network.

Where practical, at least 1 m from the back of kerb, and long side offset 350 mm from the street-facing boundary.

Long side parallel to the property boundary or roadway.

Clear of driveways and proposed driveways. Where there is no alternative, a high strength pit is used.

Not in unmade carriageways, roadways, parking bays, swales, outside doorways or in hazardous areas.

Within 6.5 m of a street conduit change of direction of 90 degrees or more.

The transformer clearance has a knock-on effect. Moving a pit along the street to clear a kiosk can leave the lots at the far end more than 25 m from a pit, which usually means an extra pit or a new pit layout. Put the electrical design's kiosk and pole positions on the plan before NBN design starts.

Bends and run lengths

  • No more than 180 degrees of bends in total between two pits. If a route needs more, a pit goes in near the middle.
  • Sharp 90 degree elbows are not allowed. P100 changes direction with 30 or 45 degree bends, or a sweeping 90 degree bend in the street network.
  • Conduit runs longer than 250 m must pass through a pit.
  • Service drops are limited to 25 m from pit to the property boundary entry.

Pit and conduit questions

What is a P20 conduit?

A 20 mm white PVC conduit. NBN uses it as the service drop from a pit to each single dwelling premises.

How close can an NBN pit be to a transformer?

NBN's standard requires pits to be at least 15 m from pad-mounted transformers and poles with transformers.

How many P20 conduits fit in a Type 5 pit?

Up to four at each end, when no P100 or only one P100 or P50 enters that end. Fewer fit when larger conduits are present.

How deep does NBN conduit need to be?

Service drops need 300 mm of cover when the run is under 3 m and 450 mm when longer. Verge conduit needs 450 mm and conduit under roads needs 600 mm.
